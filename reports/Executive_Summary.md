
# Executive Summary
## Customer Segmentation & Churn Pattern Analytics in European Banking

### 1. Project Overview

This project focuses on understanding customer churn in a European bank. Customer churn happens when customers stop using the bank's services or close their accounts.

The main purpose of this project was to study customer data, identify groups with higher churn and understand the patterns that may help banks improve customer retention.

I used Python to analyse the data and created a Streamlit dashboard to display the results.

### 2. Dataset Details

The dataset contains 10,000 customer records and 14 columns.

It includes details such as age, gender, country, credit score, account balance, salary, tenure, number of products and customer exit status.

I used this information to compare customers who left the bank with those who stayed.

### 3. Main Results

The overall results from the analysis are:

| Details | Result |
|---|---:|
| Total Customers | 10,000 |
| Customers Who Left | 2,037 |
| Customers Who Stayed | 7,963 |
| Overall Churn | 20.37% |
| Overall Retention | 79.63% |
| High-Balance Customers | 3,191 |
| High-Balance Customers Who Left | 771 |
| High-Balance Customer Churn | 24.16% |

### 4. Important Findings

- Germany had the highest churn percentage among the three countries.
- Inactive customers had a churn percentage of 26.85%, compared with 14.27% for active customers.
- High-balance customers had a churn percentage of 24.16%.
- Customers with three and four products showed very high churn percentages, although these groups had fewer customers.
- Age showed a positive relationship with customer churn.
- Salary and credit card ownership showed only small differences in churn.

### 5. Suggestions

Based on the analysis, banks can consider the following steps:

- Follow up with inactive customers.
- Understand the reasons for higher churn in Germany.
- Pay attention to customers with higher account balances.
- Provide suitable services for different age groups.
- Regularly monitor customer activity and churn percentages.

### 6. Dashboard

I also developed a Streamlit dashboard for this project.

It displays customer details, churn percentages and charts based on country, age and tenure. Users can apply filters based on country, age, gender and account activity.

### 7. Conclusion

This project helped me understand how customer data can be used to study churn patterns in banking.

Through this work, I gained practical experience in Python, data cleaning, exploratory data analysis, data visualization and Streamlit.

The findings can help banks identify customer groups that may need more attention and plan ways to improve customer retention.
